# Human vs. Algorithm: What Makes Creative Work Read as "Made by People With Taste"

*A research report for the StrikeCast team. Written to be read by anyone, technical or not.*

The trigger for this report: a bowling game's in-world scoreboard read **"HOOK · PHYSICS · BOWLING."** Someone on the team looked at it and said, in effect, *"That's something a computer would say."* They were right, and this report is about why they were right, what the deeper distinction actually is, and how to write and design so that people think *a team of real people made this* instead of *an AI did this.*

---

## 1. The core distinction, in plain terms

Here is the single most useful way to understand it:

> **An algorithm reaches for the center of the bell curve. A person with taste reaches for one specific point and leaves the rest out.**

When a language model writes, it is literally predicting the most probable next word given everything before it. That is its whole job. So its default output is, by construction, the *average* of everything ever written on a subject. Average is smooth. Average is balanced. Average offends no one and excites no one. One analysis of why AI design all looks the same puts it bluntly: the model "picks the statistically most likely layout, palette, and font pairing, so the output converges on the common choice" ([enter.converge.ai](https://enter.converge.ai/page/en-US/blog/ai-design-sameness)).

Now look at **"HOOK · PHYSICS · BOWLING"** through that lens. It fails in three specific, nameable ways:

**It's a balanced list of three with no point of view.** Three nouns, evenly weighted, separated by tidy dots. Nothing is the hero; nothing is sacrificed. A human who loves bowling doesn't experience the game as a balanced inventory of its features. They experience *one* thing most — the hush before the ball hits the pocket, the turkey, the one lane that always eats your spares. A list of three equal nouns is what you write when you have surveyed the topic, not when you have *felt* it.

**Every word is a category, not a feeling.** "Hook," "physics," "bowling" are the dictionary headwords — the most generic, most-probable terms for the subject. It's the difference between saying "Italian food" and saying "the garlic hit the pan." Research on what makes writing land emotionally is unanimous on this: concrete, specific detail is what moves people, and abstraction is what deadens them. Copyblogger argues that concrete details are what let a writer "shift readers into different emotional states" ([copyblogger.com](https://copyblogger.com/concrete-details/)); a Vanderbilt writing guide shows that a "sick woman" means nothing until you describe "a sticky film over her half-closed eyes" ([vanderbilt.edu](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/writing/)). "HOOK · PHYSICS · BOWLING" is the sticky-film sentence with the sticky film removed.

**It describes the product from the outside, like a spec sheet.** It's what a search engine would tag the game, not what a player would shout. It has the posture of a label, not a voice. Nobody talks like this. And "nobody actually talks like this" is the tell underneath all the others.

So the uncomfortable truth the stakeholder sensed: the line isn't *wrong.* It's *defensibly, inoffensively, forgettably correct.* That's exactly the smell. **Correct-but-dead is the signature of the machine.** Humans with taste produce work that is a little lopsided, a little opinionated, occasionally willing to be wrong — because they chose something.

## 2. The tells: how to recognize "a computer made this"

These are the specific, recognizable patterns. None is proof on its own — human writers use all of them sometimes, and detection tools that rely on any single one produce false positives ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/chatgpt-hypen-em-dash-ai-writing-1235314945/)). The WikiProject AI Cleanup guidance makes the same caveat: these are clusters, not fingerprints. But when several show up together, densely and at regular intervals, that's the smell.

### In writing

- **The balanced tricolon.** Three items, evenly weighted, no hierarchy. *"Fast, simple, powerful." "Hook · Physics · Bowling."* The giveaway is that you could reorder the three and nothing would change. A human ranks; a machine balances.
- **"Not just X, but Y" / "It's not X — it's Y."** The negative-parallelism move: deny one thing to elevate another, manufacturing a fake sense of revelation. *"It's not a game — it's an experience."* The Boston Globe found "not just X, but Y" in about 6% of ChatGPT chats; one critic notes it "becomes comical by the third time" ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/11/13/business/chatgpt-writing-style-clues/), [Pasquale Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/11195/new-em-dash-9-ai-writing-tells)).
- **Empty superlatives and stock adjectives.** *Seamless, powerful, intuitive, elevate, unlock, robust, cutting-edge, game-changing.* Words that assert quality instead of demonstrating it. The telltale sign: delete the adjective and you lose nothing, because it was never carrying real information.
- **Hedging and symmetry of opinion.** *"While some players prefer X, others may find Y."* The refusal to come down on one side. A person with taste has a favorite lane, a hated split, a strong opinion about house balls. The machine stays diplomatically neutral.
- **Feature-listing instead of benefit-feeling.** Reciting what a thing *has* rather than what it *does to you.* "Motion controls, real physics, multiplayer" vs. "you'll feel the ball leave your hand."
- **The em-dash tic and stacked fragments.** ChatGPT's em-dash use exploded in 2025 — from under 1 in 10 responses to more than half by summer ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/11/13/business/chatgpt-writing-style-clues/)). Also the clipped-fragment cadence: *"Fast. Simple. Yours."* Rhythmically tidy, humanly hollow.
- **Over-explaining the obvious and tidy summary bows.** *"In conclusion, bowling is a fun activity enjoyed by many."* Nobody needed telling.
- **No insider knowledge.** The surest tell of all. The writing knows *about* the subject but was never *inside* it. It says "three strikes in a row" where a bowler says "turkey."

### In visual / design work

- **Soft gradients, rounded cards, a purple-to-blue hero, a safe sans-serif.** A Dev.to writer catalogued this as the default "AI website" look; Repaint notes the "AI aesthetic" is now its own recognizable, interchangeable genre ([repaint.com](https://repaint.com/blog/picture-is-worth-a-thousand-tokens)).
- **Safe symmetry and centered everything.** Perfectly balanced layouts with no focal tension. Real designers break the grid on purpose to make your eye go *somewhere.*
- **Corporate blue + an orbital swoosh logo.** The single most-cloned identity in tech ([bd2.com](https://bd2.com/insights/have-you-noticed-that-ai-is-making-everything-look-the-same/)).
- **Polished but interchangeable.** Venngage's definition of design slop: "polished but feel[s] interchangeable," with reused boxed-layout templates across unrelated projects ([venngage.com](https://venngage.com/blog/ai-slop-in-design/)). High finish, zero fingerprint.

## 3. The human-craft playbook

Each of these is a move you can actually make. Before/after included.

**1. Pick one idea and sacrifice the other two.**
A single strong thought beats a balanced list every time. Choose the hill.
- Before: `HOOK · PHYSICS · BOWLING`
- After: `IT HOOKS IF YOU MEAN IT`

**2. Trade the category word for the telling detail.**
Specificity creates emotion — this is the whole ballgame ([Author Magazine](https://authormagazine.org/editorsblog/4746), [copyblogger.com](https://copyblogger.com/concrete-details/)). Name the thing, not the kind of thing.
- Before: `Experience realistic ball physics.`
- After: `Watch it read the oil and come back for the pocket.`

**3. Have an opinion. Risk being disagreed with.**
Taste means somebody could object. Neutral copy is machine copy.
- Before: `Various lane conditions available.`
- After: `We gave you the house shot. Earn the sport pattern.`

**4. Use the insider's word, not the dictionary's.**
Subculture lingo is proof a human who belongs wrote this. (The whole bowling glossary in §4 is for this.)
- Before: `You got three strikes in a row!`
- After: `TURKEY.`

**5. Write it the way a person would say it out loud.**
Read it aloud. If your mouth stumbles or your friend would never say it, rewrite. Mailchimp's guide builds its whole voice on "human, familiar, friendly, straightforward" and ranks clarity above cleverness ([styleguide.mailchimp.com](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)).
- Before: `Connection has been successfully established.`
- After: `You're in. Phone's the ball now.`

**6. Confident omission — cut the obvious.**
Trust the reader. The space you leave is doing work. This is the restraint principle: error states and money moments get terse and serious ([Guidewire voice guidelines]), celebration gets loud, and in all cases you say *less* than you're tempted to.
- Before: `Please wait while we load your game. This may take a few moments.`
- After: `Oiling the lane…`

**7. Earn humor; don't force it, and know when to shut up.**
Wit makes a brand memorable (Sitepoint praises Oatly's cookie popup for exactly this), but a forced joke is worse than none, and in error or money moments you keep a straight face ([sitepoint.com](https://www.sitepoint.com/writing-clear-human-friendly-microcopy/), [styleguide.mailchimp.com](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)).
- Before: `Oops! Something went wrong. 😅`
- After (on a dropped connection): `Lost the pin signal. Give it a sec.`

**8. Let in intentional imperfection.**
The Japanese aesthetic of *wabi-sabi* prizes the handmade flaw — the slightly-off thing reads as alive precisely because a machine would have smoothed it out. In copy, that's a sentence fragment, a lowercase shout, a line that's a beat too long because that's how the joke lands. Perfect symmetry is the tell; a deliberate wobble is the signature.

**9. Show, don't tell — demonstrate the quality instead of claiming it.**
Don't say "powerful." Make the player *feel* power. Don't say "fun." Be fun.
- Before: `An immersive, next-gen bowling experience.`
- After: `Nine feet of hardwood between your couch and a 300.`

**10. Reference the real culture.**
Nod to things only people in the world would recognize — league night, rental shoes, the vending-machine nachos. Cultural reference is a handshake between two humans.

## 4. The bowling vocabulary (so copy sounds like a bowler, not a dictionary)

Verified against the United States Bowling Congress glossary and Bowling.com. This is your palette. Using it correctly is itself the strongest anti-AI signal, because it proves a person who *bowls* wrote the game.

- **Turkey** — three strikes in a row. (Tradition: alleys once gave away a turkey for three in a row.) Four is a **hambone** or **four-bagger**; bowlers talk about "bagging" strikes. ([bowl.com](https://bowl.com/welcome/bowling-lingo), [bowling.com](https://www.bowling.com/knowledge-hub/bowling-tips/bowling-terms-glossary-learning-the-lingo-of-the-lanes))
- **The pocket** — the sweet spot you aim for: between the 1 and 3 pins for a righty, the 1 and 2 for a lefty. A **flush** hit is "solid in the pocket." ([bowl.com](https://bowl.com/welcome/bowling-lingo))
- **Brooklyn** — a strike that crosses to the *wrong* side of the headpin and goes in anyway. Lucky, slightly embarrassing, totally counts. ([bowling.com](https://www.bowling.com/knowledge-hub/bowling-tips/bowling-terms-glossary-learning-the-lingo-of-the-lanes))
- **Tap** — a perfect-looking pocket hit that still leaves one pin standing for no fair reason. The universal heartbreak. ([rabs.statenweb.com](https://rabs.statenweb.com/))
- **Ringing ten** (ring 10) — pin action spins a pin *around* the 10 pin without knocking it down. A specific, maddening flavor of tap. ([bowl.com](https://bowl.com/welcome/bowling-lingo))
- **Carry** — how well your ball "carries" the pins: does it drive through and send them flying, or leave stragglers. Good carry = more strikes. ([bowling.com](https://www.bowling.com/knowledge-hub/questions/bowling-tips/bowling-terms/question_132_what-does-carry-mean-in-bowling))
- **Revs** — revolutions, the number of times the ball rolls over from your hand to the pins. More revs = more hook. ([bowl.com](https://bowl.com/welcome/bowling-lingo))
- **Hook** — the ball's curve. "Throwing a boomer" = a big hooking ball.
- **The house shot** — the forgiving, oiled-in-the-middle lane pattern a regular alley uses; it funnels the ball toward the pocket. The **sport shot / sport pattern** is the brutal, even-oil tournament condition that "largely eliminates the funnel" ([Wikipedia: Sport bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_bowling)).
- **Sandbagging** — deliberately bowling badly to keep your average low so you clean up on handicap later. The oldest hustle in the house ([11thframe.com](https://www.11thframe.com/news/article/6499/Biggest-State-Tournament-in-country-cant-escape-bowlings-broken-nature), [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/sandbag)).
- **Pot game / action** — bowling for money, usually one-on-one. "Action" is the USBC's own word for "bowling for money." ([bowl.com](https://bowl.com/welcome/bowling-lingo))
- **Throw it in the ditch** — put it in the **channel** (the gutter), where you score zero. ([bowl.com](https://bowl.com/welcome/bowling-lingo))
- Good extras: **clean game** (no open frames), **deuce** (a 200 game), **Dutch 200** (alternating strikes and spares to exactly 200), **bedposts** (the 7–10 split), **the big four** (4-6-7-10), **Greek church**, **mother-in-law** (the 7 pin), **sleeper** (a pin hiding directly behind another), **bucket**, **washout**, **throwing rocks** (piling up strikes with a fast ball). ([bowl.com](https://bowl.com/welcome/bowling-lingo), [bowling.com](https://www.bowling.com/knowledge-hub/bowling-tips/bowling-terms-glossary-learning-the-lingo-of-the-lanes))

## 5. Applied to StrikeCast

### 5a. Replacements for "HOOK · PHYSICS · BOWLING"

Pick one with a point of view. My ranked favorites first.

1. **PUT SOME REVS ON IT.** — an instruction and a dare, pure insider.
2. **YOUR LIVING ROOM IS NOW LANE 7.** — the whole promise in one concrete image.
3. **THROW IT LIKE YOU MEAN IT.** — attitude, not spec sheet.
4. **NINE FEET OF HARDWOOD. ONE COUCH. ZERO EXCUSES.** — the imperfect, slightly-too-long human line.
5. **READ THE OIL. FIND THE POCKET.** — speaks fluent bowler.
6. **NO SHOES REQUIRED.** — the wink only a bowler fully gets (rental shoes).
7. **HOOK IT. CARRY IT. BAG THE TURKEY.** — if you want the rhythm of three, make them *verbs* and make them *lingo*, so it's a hype chant, not an index.
8. **THE HOUSE SHOT IS JUST THE WARM-UP.** — implies depth and has an opinion.

### 5b. UI microcopy: generic → human

Format: ~~generic~~ → **human**

**Loading / into a match**
~~Loading your game, please wait...~~ → **Oiling the lane…**

**Connecting the phone controller**
~~Establishing connection to your device.~~ → **Grab your phone. That's the ball now.**

**Pairing instructions**
~~Please ensure your phone and TV are on the same Wi-Fi network, then scan the QR code to connect.~~ → **Same Wi-Fi, phone and TV. Point the camera here. Wind up.**

**Strike call-out**
~~Congratulations! You scored a strike!~~ → **FLUSH IN THE POCKET. 🎳**

**Three strikes in a row**
~~You got three strikes in a row!~~ → **TURKEY. Somebody's hot.**

**Spare**
~~Nice spare!~~ → **Picked it clean.**

**A tap (great shot, one pin left)**
~~You left one pin standing.~~ → **That's a tap. Robbed. Ten pin's still laughing.**

**Gutter ball**
~~Gutter ball. Score: 0.~~ → **Straight in the ditch. Happens to everyone. Once.**

**Empty state — no friends online**
~~You have no active multiplayer sessions.~~ → **Nobody on the lanes yet. Text someone. Loser buys nachos.**

**Empty state — no games played**
~~No game history to display.~~ → **Your scorecard's blank. Let's fix that.**

**Calibration / aim check**
~~Calibrating motion sensors. Please hold still.~~ → **Hold steady — reading your release.**

**Reconnecting after a drop**
~~Connection lost. Attempting to reconnect.~~ → **Lost the pin signal. Give it a sec.**

**Turn prompt**
~~It is now your turn to bowl.~~ → **You're up. Lane's yours.**

**Final frame / clean game**
~~You completed the game with no open frames.~~ → **Clean game. Not a single open frame. Respect.**

**Settings — difficulty**
~~Select lane difficulty: Easy / Medium / Hard~~ → **Pick your oil: House Shot / Sport Shot / Good Luck**

## 6. The "did a computer make this?" checklist

Run any line of copy or any design choice through these. A "no" on the first five is the fire alarm.

1. **Does it have a point of view?** Could a reasonable person disagree with it? If it's perfectly balanced and offends no one, a machine would write it. Rank; don't list.
2. **Is there one concrete, specific detail** — a real noun, a real moment — instead of a category word? ("The pocket," not "bowling.")
3. **Would an actual person say this out loud?** Read it aloud. If your mouth trips or your friend never would, rewrite.
4. **Does it use the insider's word** where an outsider would use the dictionary's? ("Turkey," not "three strikes.")
5. **Did we cut something we were tempted to keep?** Confident omission. If every obvious thing is explained, it's padded.
6. **Scan for the tells:** balanced tricolon, "not just X but Y," empty superlatives (seamless/powerful/elevate/unlock), hedging, feature-listing, tidy summary bow, em-dash pile-up. Any cluster = rewrite.
7. **Design: is anything deliberately off-center or imperfect** to make the eye move — or is it all safe symmetry, soft gradient, corporate blue, interchangeable polish?
8. **The gut test:** does it feel like *a specific person who loves this thing* made it — or like it was assembled from the average of everything ever written about it?

---

### One sentence to tape to the wall
**The machine writes the most likely thing; a person with taste writes the one specific, lopsided, lived-in thing — and throws the rest in the ditch.**
